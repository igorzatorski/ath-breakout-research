## Current hypothesis and scope

Does a close above the true prior all-history HIGH contain incremental
information about subsequent stock returns? Compare with same-window SPY and
same-date non-ATH stocks, then examine momentum and market-beta explanations.
This remains an event study, not a completed portfolio strategy.

## What exists

- Modular local Python research code, fixed protocols, saved event/control IDs,
  return paths, coverage, SHA256 manifests, tests, and an experiment register.
- Local CRSP total-return history 1993 through June 2026; signals from 1994;
  historical monthly liquidity universe and $10m ADV21 threshold. The historical
  population is roughly 3,000 liquid stocks, not all US equities or current-only
  index constituents. 75,884 history-quality-qualified signals.
- Corrected pre-1993 maximum HIGH seed, conservative uncertain-history exclusions,
  next-open execution, distributions, CIZ terminal once and subsequent cash.
- Signal horizons 5, 21, 63, 126, 252, 504, 756 sessions; same-date SPY, five

## What the evidence says

1. Short-horizon baseline: 21-session matched sample 72,023; excess SPY +0.27 pp
   and similar-stock controls +0.26 pp, intervals including zero. Suppressing
   the next 21 sessions after each accepted signal leaves control excess near
   +0.25 pp; that alone does not establish independent observations.
2. Full annual stock/SPY sample 71,961: ATH +12.64%, SPY +10.09%, excess +2.55 pp;
   interval -1.99 to +8.82 pp. Mean excess changes sign by period; annual median
   paired excess is -3.40 pp. Recurring events and positive tails matter.
3. On 65,434 identical events across all seven horizons, largest tested mean
   SPY excess occurs at 252 sessions (+3.40 pp), then +1.22 pp at 504 and -1.59 pp
   at 756. This is descriptive peak selection, not a validated optimal exit.
   Median three-year closing drawdown is -48.62%, versus SPY -24.50%.
4. Momentum: same 43,824 events, annual control excess +2.29 pp without momentum
   matching versus +1.80 pp with it. Paired change -0.49 pp is uncertain. New
   excess's date-block interval is positive, but equal-security excess -4.68 pp.
5. CAPM: all available 67,213 ATH CARs average +2.33 pp; interval includes zero.
   Common 39,561: ATH CAR +1.60 pp, momentum-control CAR -0.64 pp, difference
   +2.24 pp with interval +1.14 to +3.50 pp. Equal-security difference -4.39 pp.
   Thus market-beta adjustment does not erase event-weighted relative association,
   but standalone CAPM outperformance is not established and weighting remains
   unresolved. These CARs sum 251 full daily residuals after entry close; they
   are not interchangeable with the earlier 252-session compounded excess.

These figures use different explicitly documented samples. Do not compare a
full-sample old mean with a reduced-sample new mean as if the change were solely
caused by a new control. Positive relative performance can accompany negative
standalone abnormal performance, especially in the recent CAPM period.

## What remains unknown

- Whether the incremental ATH association survives company-level dependence,
  fair treatment of repeated signals, concentration and tail contributions.
- Whether the result is shared across PERMNO/PERMCO, sectors and periods, or
  dominated by a few firms or the history-quality/matching exclusions.
- Whether value, size, profitability, investment and momentum factor exposures
  explain apparent CAPM abnormal returns; beta itself may change after ATH.
- Whether similar abnormal performance already existed before ATH; pre-trend
  and placebo-date evidence is still needed to attribute incremental signal value.
- Whether any fixed decision rule has positive net performance after spreads,
  slippage, turnover, feasible capital and risk constraints.
- Whether a fixed rule generalizes to untouched later data. Already inspected
  2018-2026 history is not a fresh holdout; the 2025 annual cohort is partial.
- Which entry filters/exits or portfolio construction choices are useful. No
  stable optimum, consolidated-base effect or causal alpha has been established.

## Ordered research stages — recommendation, not execution authorization

1. **Next: resolve observation weighting and concentration.** On the same frozen
   matchable annual sample, quantify event counts and excess contributions by
   PERMNO/PERMCO; show top contributors without deleting them to improve results.
   Compare one fixed non-overlapping event-per-company sampling rule selected
   only from past signal dates, and uncertainty that handles company as well as
   calendar dependence. Preserve the original event-weighted estimand and report
   what changes when the estimand becomes typical-company or calendar exposure.
2. **Then factor robustness.** If the association is sufficiently broad to justify
   continuation, freeze one expanded model (e.g. market/size/value/momentum),
   compare exactly the same events, and include uncertainty/model limitations.
   These are risk controls, not a search for the specification with best alpha.
3. **Only then a single economic extension.** If evidence supports it, predeclare
   one filter hypothesis (e.g. consolidation), one definition and one comparison;
   keep entries/horizon/other rules fixed. A null broad ATH result is an acceptable
   outcome and does not justify an unrestricted grid search to rescue it.
4. **Independent validation.** Freeze the rule and data policy; test chronologically
   on genuinely uninspected/new data. Use known-history walk-forward only as a
   retrospective diagnostic, not a substitute for untouched validation.
5. **Portfolio and execution.** After a defensible signal, choose fixed allocation,
   exits, capital limits and costs and assess risk-adjusted investable returns.
   This is where strategy-level Jensen/factor alpha and implementability belong.

Current recommendation: do stage 1 next. Do not start consolidation/filter
optimization or choose one-year exits merely because that horizon looked best.
